# How to Brief a Logo Designer: A Field Guide for New Businesses in the UAE

A logo is usually the first piece of branding a new company pays for, and it often happens in a rush: the trade licence is approved, the shop sign has to be ordered, and someone needs a file by Thursday. Rushed logos tend to be replaced within two years, which means paying twice and confusing customers in between.

The good news is that most of the work that makes a logo succeed happens before a designer opens any software. This guide explains what to prepare, what to ask for and how to judge the concepts you receive.

## Start with the business, not the picture

Designers are good at drawing; they cannot guess your strategy. Before you request a quote, write half a page answering these questions:

- What does the company do, in one sentence a stranger would understand?
- Who are the customers? Families, contractors, hotel procurement teams, tourists?
- Who are your three closest competitors, and how do you want to look different from them?
- Three words that describe the personality you want (for example: precise, warm, premium).
- Where will the logo appear most often? A mobile app icon, embroidered uniforms, a vehicle wrap and an Instagram profile have very different needs.

This short document becomes your creative brief. It also protects you: when you review concepts later, you judge them against the brief rather than against personal taste.

## Plan for two scripts from day one

Many businesses in the UAE use both English and Arabic. If there is any chance you will need an Arabic version of your name, mention it at the start. A good bilingual logo is designed so that both scripts feel balanced in weight and height; bolting Arabic lettering under an English wordmark later rarely looks right.

Ask the designer whether they work with native Arabic typographers or licensed Arabic typefaces, and request to see bilingual work they have done before.

## Understand what you are buying

Logo packages vary a lot. When comparing offers, check exactly what is delivered:

1. Number of initial concepts (two or three strong directions is usually more useful than ten weak ones)
2. Number of revision rounds included
3. File formats: you need vector files (SVG, AI or EPS) as well as PNG and JPG
4. Colour versions: full colour, single colour, black and white, and a reversed version for dark backgrounds
5. Layout variants: horizontal, stacked and an icon-only mark for small spaces
6. A simple brand sheet listing colour codes (HEX, RGB, CMYK and ideally Pantone) and fonts
7. Transfer of copyright to your company once paid

If a package does not include vector files and copyright transfer, treat it as incomplete, however cheap it is.

## Keep it simple enough to survive

Strong logos work at the size of a fingernail and on the side of a building. Try these practical tests on every concept:

- **Shrink test:** view it at 32 pixels wide. Can you still recognise it?
- **Mono test:** print it in black only. Does it still work without colour?
- **Distance test:** look at it from across the room. Is the name readable?
- **Context test:** place it on a mock-up of your shopfront, website header and business card.

Gradients, fine lines and tiny details often fail these tests. They can still be used in campaigns, but the core logo should be robust.

## Check trademarks and similarity

Before you fall in love with a concept, search for similar marks in your industry. In the UAE, trademarks are registered with the Ministry of Economy, and a quick search of existing registrations can save you from a costly rebrand later. Ask your designer to confirm the work is original and does not rely on stock icons that thousands of other businesses can also download.

## Choosing a designer

You can work with a freelancer, a branding studio or a digital agency that also builds websites. A combined provider can be convenient because the logo, website and social media templates are designed together and stay consistent. If you are comparing agencies that offer [Logo Design Dubai](https://budgetweb.ae/) services, ask to see complete identity projects rather than isolated logos, so you can judge how well their marks work in real use.

Whoever you choose, look for:

- A portfolio with variety, not one recognisable house style applied to everyone
- A clear process with defined stages and approval points
- Written terms covering revisions, timelines, payment and ownership

## Giving useful feedback

Feedback such as "make it pop" or "I don't like it" slows everything down. Be specific and link comments back to the brief: "The icon feels playful, but our customers are law firms, so it should feel more formal" gives the designer something to act on. Collect opinions from your partners first and send one consolidated set of comments per round.

## After approval

Store the final files in a shared company drive, not on one person's laptop. Send the brand sheet to anyone producing material for you — printers, signage companies, social media managers — so that colours and spacing stay consistent everywhere.

A logo will not make a business successful on its own, but a well-briefed, well-built one quietly does its job for a decade. That makes the few hours you spend preparing one of the best investments a new company can make.
